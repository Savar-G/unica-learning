Set 1: pre-trial views

On the pre-trial pages, where does the one action sit relative to the report, so a BC parent gets from a finished report to Start free month in the fewest clicks with the report in view?

Varies: placement of the next-step action relative to the report (information hierarchy). Decides it: Clicks and scrolling from opening the dashboard to the Stripe page; whether the verdict is still the first thing read (D-084: say it plainly, never to sell); whether the email's ?step=plan link lands on the button.

test_running and test_waiting are the two halves of the spec's test_running stage (test open; finished with the report not ready). Open a state with the address hash, for example pre-b.html#report_ready_waitlist. Option C also takes #report_ready_bc&open to show the full report unfolded. The -scrolled screenshots show the report 2200 px down: the action is in view there only in B (A shows the struggles; C's full report is folded, so it has none).

Option A

Action card above the report

Show: No testTest openReport being madeReport, BCReport, outside BC Open full size

Bet
Parents read top to bottom: put the next step once at the top, then the whole report, and repeat it in the report's own Next step row at the end.
Benefit
Simplest to build: the current ReportCard plus one card. One click from the first screen and from the end. Same on desktop and phone.
Cost
In the middle of the report the button is out of view, so a parent who decides while reading scrolls up or down for it.
Failure risk
The price card sits above the verdict, so the page opens on a sales line before the result. That works against D-084 and may read as pushy.

Option B

Report with a next-step rail

Recommended

Show: No testTest openReport being madeReport, BCReport, outside BC Open full size

Bet
The decision happens while reading, at no fixed point: keep the action beside the report at every scroll depth, and let the report itself open the page.
Benefit
One click from anywhere in the report, with the verdict still first. The rail has room for what happens after the click (Workbook 1, mailing, when the free month starts) and the optional learning questions. On a phone it becomes one button fixed at the bottom.
Cost
The report column is about 830 px wide at 1440, so its rows stack (title above content), as the report already does on tablets. That needs a container rule in the report CSS. The fixed phone button covers about 90 px of the report.
Failure risk
A rail that never leaves can feel like a sales sidebar. The words stay the report's own and plain, with no urgency, to keep it informational.

Option C

Result and action first, full report below

Bet
Most parents decide on the headline: show the report's level band and the action side by side in the first screen, and fold the rest of the report under Read the full report.
Benefit
The shortest path: on desktop the level, the verdict and the button share the first screen, with no fixed bar. On a 390 x 844 phone the button sits just below the first screen (about 880 px down), one short scroll.
Cost
The areas, strengths, struggles and the child's work are one click away. The page needs a second state (the unfolded report) and the email link must unfold it.
Failure risk
Story 13 (see the report at once) and the report's job of showing the gaps: a parent may feel the detail is hidden behind the sale, and the button may look like the point of the page.

Recommendation

B. The job is the fewest clicks with the report in view: B is the only option where the button is one click away at every point of the report while the verdict, not the price, opens the page. A is the fallback if the container rule for the narrower report is not wanted. C is the fastest path, but it hides the detail that makes the report worth trusting.

Set 2: phone layout of the shell

On a phone, how do the seven tabs fit around the decided bottom bar (Overview, Progress, Scan pages, Workbooks, More), and where do the child switcher, Open the desk and the family Account go?

Varies: where the overflow tabs and family controls live (navigation model). Decides it: Taps to reach Maya, Assessment and Account (cancel, invoices); whether the child switcher and Open the desk are where a parent looks; how the bar behaves today, when Progress, Scan pages and Maya are still hidden.

#overview is the active stage with all seven tabs (future, Sample data). #more opens More. #today is first_book_coming as it can ship now: Overview, Workbooks, Assessment, Account (Progress waits for #194, Scan pages for #457, Maya for #337). The desktop screenshot is the same mock sidebar in all three.

Option A

More opens a sheet

Recommended

Show: All seven tabsMore openToday's tabs Open full size

Bet
The familiar phone pattern: four daily tabs in the bar, and More opens a sheet with Maya, Assessment and Account. The child block with Open the desk sits at the top of each page.
Benefit
Every tab is at most two taps. Nothing new to learn. With five tabs or fewer there is no More: today's bar is simply Overview, Workbooks, Assessment, Account.
Cost
A bottom sheet is a new component (focus, Escape, swipe to close, back button). The child switcher scrolls away with the page.
Failure risk
Account (cancel, invoices, delete) is two taps down on a phone, behind a word that says nothing.

Option B

More is a page for the child and the family

Show: All seven tabsMore openToday's tabs Open full size

Bet
More is not an overflow menu but a home for everything that is not daily: the children (switch, Open the desk), the child's Maya and Assessment, and the Account sections listed directly.
Benefit
Account's sections (plans and invoices, card, pause or cancel, children, delete) are one tap from More, with no extra Account page. No overlay to build.
Cost
The More page repeats the Account tab of the desktop in another shape, so two layouts of the same content must stay in step. Today More appears even with four tabs.
Failure risk
The child switcher is mostly in More; a parent of two may not find it from Overview, and the page mixes child and family items.

Option C

Family controls in the top bar

Show: All seven tabsMore openToday's tabs Open full size

Bet
Split by scope: everything in the bottom bar is about the selected child; the family (the child switcher and Account) sits in a top bar that stays. More holds only Maya and Assessment.
Benefit
Matches the spec's rule that Account covers the family, not one child. Account and the switcher are one tap from every page. More is a small menu of two.
Cost
Two navigation places to learn. The top bar keeps 56 px of a small screen on every page. Today the bar has only three tabs.
Failure risk
Parents look for cancel in More, not behind an Account icon at the top; and an always-visible switcher adds weight for one-child families (most families).

Recommendation

A. It keeps the decided bar, needs no second copy of Account, and falls back cleanly today, when only four tabs exist and no More is needed. If Savar wants, take one part of C into it: keep the child switcher fixed in the top bar for families with two or more children.
