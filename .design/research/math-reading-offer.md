# What "math and reading" means at Pagewise (ages 6-12)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Sources: BC curriculum (curriculum.gov.bc.ca), Ontario curriculum (dcp.edu.gov.on.ca), Common Core (thecorestandards.org), NAEP 2024, EQAO 2024-25, BC FSA open data, NMAP 2008, Gallup/Learning Heroes 2023, and 25+ competitor landing pages. Items marked [unverified] came from search snippets only.

## Summary

1. The site says "math and reading" and "follows the BC curriculum" but never names a skill. Every serious competitor names skills somewhere (Kumon, Mathnasium, Beast Academy, IXL, Brighterly, Lovevery).
2. Homepages stay vague; the skill list lives one layer down (program page, level page, or a section). A short named-skill list per age band is the norm. Nobody shows a full scope and sequence on the landing page.
3. Placement first is universal. Pagewise already leads with the free assessment, so this is table stakes, not a differentiator.
4. Physical products show a real page: Kumon (worksheet PDF), Beast Academy (sample pages per unit), Popular Book ("real pages"), Spectrum ("Look inside"). Pagewise has page mocks already; it has no "see a sample page" moment.
5. Only IXL says it aligns to the BC curriculum outright; Mathnasium BC hedges ("can align... when appropriate"); JUMP Math names BC among five provinces. Nobody combines physical + personalized + named skills + BC mapping. That combination is the gap.
6. BC math is specific and easy to map to. BC reading is not: the standard says only "reading strategies", and its elaborations teach three-cueing ("meaning, structure, and visual"), which the science-of-reading movement and Ontario's 2023 curriculum reject. "Follows the BC curriculum" is honest for math and weak for reading.
7. BC is slow on math facts: multiplication facts are "not intended" for memorization in Grade 3, only 2s/5s/10s by Grade 4. Ontario wants 10x10 by Grade 4; Common Core by Grade 3. Parents notice this gap.
8. The data says the pain is real: about 41% of BC Grade 7 writers are "Emerging" in numeracy (FSA 2025/26); Ontario Grade 6 math is 51% at standard; fractions are the "major obstacle" to algebra (NMAP 2008). Early reading losses are largest in K-1 (Curriculum Associates 2025).
9. Parents do not know their child is behind: 88-89% say their child is at or above grade level (Gallup/Learning Heroes 2023). Copy that names skills lets a parent self-diagnose ("oh, she can't do that yet").
10. Best pattern to copy is Lovevery's "Inside the kit": each item is named, with one plain line on the skill it builds, plus "You don't need to know how to teach math."

## Recommendation

Both: a new section, plus two FAQ fixes.

- **New section: "What your child will work on."** Two tabs (Math, Reading) and three age bands (6-7, 8-9, 10-12, with grades in small type). Each band has 5-7 plain skill lines. End with a link to a sample page. Place it right after "How it works", where the parent has just seen the loop and asks "but what's in the book?"
- **FAQ:** rewrite "Does it follow the BC curriculum?" with the honest wording below, and add "What will my child actually work on?" with a two-sentence answer that links to the section.
- **Hero chips:** use the list at the end. Lead with the high-anxiety skills (fractions, times tables, phonics).

## Draft section copy

**Eyebrow:** WHAT'S IN THE BOOKS
**Headline:** What your child will work on.
**Lead:** The skills your child learns at school, from first sounds to fractions. Each book picks the ones your child is ready for next.

### Math

**Ages 6-7** (Grades 1-2)
- Numbers to 100, and what each digit means
- Adding and subtracting to 20, from memory
- Adding and subtracting to 100
- Skip-counting by 2s, 5s and 10s
- Coins, shapes and patterns
- Short word problems

**Ages 8-9** (Grades 3-4)
- Numbers to 10,000
- Times tables and division facts
- What fractions are, and how to compare them
- Decimals to hundredths
- Telling time, money and perimeter
- Two-step word problems

**Ages 10-12** (Grades 5-7)
- Multiplying big numbers and long division
- Equivalent fractions and mixed numbers
- Fractions, decimals and percents, and how they connect
- Order of operations, ratios and negative numbers
- Area, volume and angles
- Budgets, sales tax and discounts

### Reading

**Ages 6-7** (Grades 1-2)
- Letter sounds, and blending them into words
- Phonics patterns, step by step (short vowels, silent e, vowel teams)
- Sight words
- Reading short books aloud, smoothly
- Retelling a story in order
- Writing simple sentences

**Ages 8-9** (Grades 3-4)
- Longer words: prefixes, suffixes and word families
- Reading aloud with expression
- New vocabulary
- Main idea and key details
- Characters, setting and plot
- Writing a paragraph

**Ages 10-12** (Grades 5-7)
- Word roots and harder vocabulary
- Reading between the lines (inference)
- Summarizing and finding the theme
- Point of view, and reading nonfiction
- Grammar: tenses, agreement, run-ons
- Writing reports and arguments

**Closing line:** Two kids the same age get two different books. *See a sample page →*

## Honest curriculum wording

Current FAQ: "Yes. Every book follows the BC curriculum for your child's grade, and starts from where your child actually is."

Proposed:

> **Does it follow the BC curriculum?**
> Yes for math: every math book covers what BC expects for your child's grade, and adds extra practice on math facts. Reading covers the BC reading and writing goals, taught with step-by-step phonics. Every book starts from where your child actually is, not just their grade.

Only ship this if the content pipeline really maps each book to BC learning standards. If it does not yet, use "Built around what your child learns at school in BC" until it does.

New FAQ:

> **What will my child actually work on?**
> In math: number sense, math facts, fractions, decimals, measurement and word problems. In reading: phonics, fluency, vocabulary and comprehension. See the full list by age above.

## Key data

- BC FSA 2025/26, on track or extending (of writers): G4 literacy 72.7%, G4 numeracy 66.5%, G7 literacy 75.3%, G7 numeracy 59.2%.
- EQAO 2024-25 (Ontario, at standard): G3 math 64%, G6 math 51%, G3 reading 74%.
- NAEP 2024 (US): G4 reading below Basic about 40%, the most since 2002; G8 math below Basic nearly 40%.
- NMAP 2008: "Difficulty with fractions (including decimals and percents) is pervasive and is a major obstacle to further progress in mathematics."
- Gallup/Learning Heroes 2023: 88% of parents say their child is at or above grade level in reading, 89% in math.
