## Status of the curriculum (checked 2026-10-15)
- The K-9 curriculum was redesigned and implemented in September 2016 (grades 10-12 later). No revised or draft K-9 math curriculum for 2025-26 or 2026-27 was found on curriculum.gov.bc.ca, news.gov.bc.ca, or in news search. The math page carries no draft or revision notice.
- March 2026: Education Minister Lisa Beare defended the existing curriculum (Global News, 2026-03-04, updated 2026-06-24) after a C.D. Howe report by Anna Stokke said BC math scores fell 42 points since 2003 and called the outcomes "very vague". No review was announced in that article.
- Related, but not a curriculum change: BC released "Learning Pathways" literacy and numeracy teaching resources in June 2025 (The Tyee, 2025-10-07); the FSA moved from spring to fall (2026-27 window 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-06).
- Not verified: any non-public ministry review. Re-check before launch.
- Useful quote in the official text: Grade 3 "Memorization of facts is not intended for this level"; Grade 4 "Students should be able to recall the following multiplication facts by the end of Grade 4 (2s, 5s, 10s)"; Grade 5 "should be able to recall many multiplication facts by the end of Grade 5 (e.g., 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, 10s)"; Grade 3 "At the end of Grade 3, most students should be able to recall addition facts to 20."

## Curricular Competencies (shared across grades; wording varies slightly by grade)
Four groups, from the Grade 1 and Grade 7 pages:
- Reasoning and analyzing: use reasoning/logic to explore and make connections; estimate reasonably; develop and apply mental math strategies; use technology/tools to explore patterns and test conjectures; model mathematics in contextualized experiences.
- Understanding and solving: develop and apply understanding through play, inquiry and problem solving; visualize; develop and use multiple strategies (Grade 7: apply multiple strategies in abstract and contextualized situations); engage in problems connected to place, story, cultural practices and local First Peoples communities.
- Communicating and representing: communicate mathematical thinking in many ways; use mathematical vocabulary; explain and justify ideas and decisions; represent ideas in concrete, pictorial, and symbolic forms.
- Connecting and reflecting: reflect on mathematical thinking (Grade 7: share thinking of self and others, evaluate strategies and solutions, extend, and pose further problems); connect concepts to each other and to other areas and personal interests; incorporate First Peoples worldviews.


## Grade 1

### Big Ideas

- Numbers to 20 represent quantities that can be decomposed into 10s and 1s.  
  _(Number: Number represents and describes quantity.)_
- Addition and subtraction with numbers to 10 can be modelled concretely, pictorially, and symbolically to develop computational fluency.  
  _(Computational Fluency: Computational fluency develops from a strong sense of number.)_
- Repeating elements in patterns can be identified.  
  _(Patterning: We use patterns to represent identified regularities and to make generalizations.)_
- Objects and shapes have attributes that can be described, measured, and compared.  
  _(Geometry and Measurement: We can describe, measure, and compare spatial relationships.)_
- Concrete graphs help us to compare and interpret data and show one-to-one correspondence.  
  _(Data and Probability: Analyzing data and chance enables us to compare and interpret.)_

## Grade 3

### Big Ideas

- Fractions are a type of number that can represent quantities.  
  _(Number: Number represents and describes quantity.)_
- Development of computational fluency in addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of whole numbers requires flexible decomposing and composing.  
  _(Computational Fluency: Computational fluency develops from a strong sense of number.)_
- Regular increases and decreases in patterns can be identified and used to make generalizations.  
  _(Patterning: We use patterns to represent identified regularities and to make generalizations.)_
- Standard units are used to describe, measure, and compare attributes of objects’ shapes.  
  _(Geometry and Measurement: We can describe, measure, and compare spatial relationships.)_
- The likelihood of possible outcomes can be examined, compared, and interpreted.  
  _(Data and Probability: Analyzing data and chance enables us to compare and interpret.)_

  _(Number: Number represents and describes quantity.)_
- Development of computational fluency and multiplicative thinking requires analysis of patterns and relations in multiplication and division.

## Notes and anomalies
- Grade 5 "decimals to thousandths" and "equivalent fractions" have no elaboration of their own on the live site; the Grade 5 "whole-number, fraction, and decimal benchmarks" elaboration carries equivalent fractions, compare/order of fractions and decimals, and decimal add/subtract.
- Grade 5 elaboration for "addition and subtraction of whole numbers to 1 000 000" says "estimating sums and differences to 10 000" (probably a carry-over; the standard says 1 000 000).
- Grade 2 "symbolic representation of equality and inequality" has no elaboration.
- Fractions: operations on fractions (add/subtract unlike denominators etc.) do NOT appear in Grades 1-7 content. Grade 3 introduces fraction concepts; Grade 4 compares fractions with common denominators; Grade 5 equivalent fractions; Grade 6 improper fractions/mixed numbers, ratios, percents. Operations with fractions arrive in Grade 8 (not checked here).
- No explicit Grade 6 or 7 standard for adding or subtracting fractions, and none for multiplying fractions. Do not generate such items for BC.
- Time: telling time is explicitly NOT expected in Grade 3; it is a Grade 4 standard.
- Multiplication facts: concepts in Grade 3, facts to 100 introduced in Grade 4 ("memorization not intended"), recall of 2s/5s/10s expected by end of Grade 4, "many" facts by end of Grade 5.

## BC Foundation Skills Assessment (FSA) numeracy, Grades 4 and 7

Sources: FSA page https://www2.gov.bc.ca/foundation-skills-assessment ; Specifications PDF (2020-21 design, "Version 1") https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/assessment/fsa_description_and_specs.pdf ; samples https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/program-management/assessment/foundation-skills-assessment/fsa-samples ; Grade 4 sample PDF https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/kindergarten-to-grade-12/assessment/gr4-fsa-sample-numeracy-en.pdf ; Grade 7 sample PDF .../gr7-fsa-sample-numeracy-en.pdf

- Who/when: all BC Grade 4 and Grade 7 students, annually. Moved from spring to fall: 2026-27 school administration 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-06, scoring deadline 2026-12-04 (FSA page, checked 2026-10-15). Results are formative, not on transcripts.
- Structure (specs Table 6/7): a 15-minute unscored group Collaboration Activity, then a paper Student Booklet and an online section.
  - Numeracy student booklet: 3 constructed-response (written) questions on real-life contexts, all Cognitive Level 3 (strategic thinking), 12 score points, 45 minutes (shared timing with literacy booklet per spec table).
  - Numeracy online: 30 selected-response questions, 60 minutes (combined with the literacy online block; spec table lists 60 minutes per online subject block). Grade 4 = 16 Level 1 (recall) + 14 Level 2 (skills and concepts); Grade 7 = 14 Level 1 + 16 Level 2. Plus a short self-reflection (1 selected-response, 1 written-response; not scored).
  - Cognitive levels use Webb's Depth of Knowledge (levels 1-3 only).
- Content weighting (Table 4): Number and Computational Fluency 35-45%; Patterns 20-25%; Geometry and Measurement 20-25%; Data and Probability 15-25%. All questions are set in real-life contexts (scenario or open-ended challenge).
- Online item types: multiple choice, drop-down, drag-and-drop, matching, scale, drawing, spatial/visual (hot spot), numerical value entry (number pad), placing items in sequence, interactive graphing.
- Sample items seen (2020/21 samples): G4 - drag base-ten blocks to show 364; "how many cards did he buy" (395 to 422); read a graph (how many more prefer peaches than pears); complete a triangle pattern by drag; about how many days is three months; pick the net for a 3D shape; work out which kits were bought with $200 and $5 left; place 300 km on a 0-1000 number line; complete the third figure of a grid pattern. G7 - enter sixty-five million three hundred thousand on a number pad; plot coordinates; 40% of 8 x $13.00 rounded to the dollar; fewest coins for $9.85; double bar graph difference; pattern table to find a time for 415 apples; 15% of 320; probability as a percent from counts; trade-value reasoning.
- Reporting: three levels Emerging, On Track, Extending (strength-based; no percentage score reported to students). Numeracy descriptors: Emerging = attempts the problem, relies on given information; On Track = solves and demonstrates the solution, uses math language accurately; Extending = solution shows reasoning, uses inference, communicates thinking clearly. Written responses use a 'Solving Real-Life Problems' rubric (Table 13 in specs).
- 2026-27 change noted on the FSA page: numeracy written-response questions "may require students to solve problems in multiple parts and steps".
- Provincial results: Grade 4 numeracy, nearly two-thirds meeting expectations 2016-17 to 2023-24, one quarter to one third not; Grade 7, over half pre-pandemic, about 40% not meeting from 2021-22 (The Tyee, 2025-10-07, https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/10/07/Math-Reading-Scores-Declining-BC/). Official 2025 provincial figures not found - mark as unverified.
- Caveat: the specs PDF on the site is dated 2021 (modified 2022-08-31) and the site says the current spec page was last updated 2025-08-29. Per-grade counts above come from that PDF; the 2026-27 form may differ.
