# BC K-8 Mathematics curriculum map for an online placement assessment

Prepared 2026-10-10 for the Pagewise online math placement assessment (grades 1-7, reporting by strand, placing a child below or above school grade, so K-8 content is mapped).

## 0. Sources and method

Primary sources (official, British Columbia Ministry of Education):

- Web, one page per grade, "Big Ideas" and "Content" with elaborations: https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/mathematics/k/core and the same pattern for /1/core to /8/core. (The Kindergarten page is /k/core. The URL /mathematics/0 returns 404.)
- PDF K-9 with elaborations: https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/curriculum/mathematics/en_mathematics_k-9_elab.pdf (footer date: June 2016).
- PDF K-9 standards only: https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/sites/curriculum.gov.bc.ca/files/curriculum/mathematics/en_mathematics_k-9.pdf

Method:

- WebFetch returns a paraphrase, so the raw HTML and PDF were downloaded and parsed locally. The standard headings below use the official PDF wording; where the PDF text was cut off by a page break (Grades 4-8), the heading comes from the matching web page. The elaboration bullets are near-verbatim and shortened from the live web pages. The PDF and the web pages agree, except one small difference in standard 5.5 (the PDF heading says add and subtract to 1 000 000; the web elaboration says estimate sums and differences to 10 000).
- Each grade has about 11-19 "Content" headings. Each heading is one learning standard. Total K-8: 131 standards. Every one has an ID (K1..K11, 1.1.., 8.14) used in section 2.
- Left out of this file: the Curricular Competencies (reasoning, problem solving, communicating, reflecting), which are the same in every grade, and the First Peoples resource and story references that sit inside the elaborations. They exist on the official pages. An online test cannot assess them directly.
- Wording rule: this file uses hyphens, never em-dashes. The official text has em-dashes in the financial literacy headings; they are shown here as " - ".

## 1. Official content standards, Kindergarten to Grade 8

Format: **ID** official standard heading, then elaboration bullets in one line (semicolon separated). The strand code in section 2 is not shown here.

### Kindergarten

Big Ideas (official):

- Numbers represent quantities that can be decomposed into smaller parts.
- One-to-one correspondence and a sense of 5 and 10 are essential for fluency with numbers.
- Repeating elements in patterns can be identified.
- Objects have attributes that can be described, measured, and compared.
- Familiar events can be described as likely or unlikely and compared.

Content (official):

- **K1** number concepts to 10. Elaboration: counting: one-to-one correspondence, conservation, cardinality, stable order counting, sequencing 1-10, linking sets to numerals, subitizing; counting collections; counting to 10 in more than one language.
- **K2** ways to make 5. Elaboration: perceptual subitizing (I see 5); conceptual subitizing (I see 4 and 1); comparing quantities 1-10; concrete materials to show ways to make 5.
- **K3** decomposition of numbers to 10. Elaboration: decompose and recompose quantities to 10; benchmarks of 5 and 10; making 10; part-part-whole thinking.
- **K4** repeating patterns with two or three elements. Elaboration: sort and classify by one attribute; identify patterns in the world; identify the core; represent patterns in various ways.
- **K5** change in quantity to 10, using concrete materials. Elaboration: generalize change by adding 1 or 2; build-and-change tasks (begin with 4 cubes, change to 6, change to 3).
- **K6** equality as a balance and inequality as an imbalance. Elaboration: pan balance with cubes on each side: equal and not equal.
- **K7** direct comparative measurement (e.g., linear, mass, capacity). Elaboration: use a baseline for linear comparison; longer/shorter/taller/wider; heavier/lighter/same as; holds more/less.
- **K8** single attributes of 2D shapes and 3D objects. Elaboration: sort by a single attribute; build and describe 3D objects; explore, create, describe 2D shapes; positional language (beside, on top of, under, in front of); math terminology not expected.
- **K9** concrete or pictorial graphs as a visual tool. Elaboration: create concrete and pictorial graphs (e.g., how students got to school) and discuss.
- **K10** likelihood of familiar life events. Elaboration: language of probability: unlikely, likely (e.g., Could it snow tomorrow?).
- **K11** financial literacy - attributes of coins, and financial role-play. Elaboration: notice colour, size, pictures of Canadian coins; name coins; role-play transactions with whole numbers (a muffin is $2.00 and a juice is $1.00); wants and needs.

### Grade 1

Big Ideas (official):

- Numbers to 20 represent quantities that can be decomposed into 10s and 1s.
- Addition and subtraction with numbers to 10 can be modelled concretely, pictorially, and symbolically to develop computational fluency.
- Repeating elements in patterns can be identified.
- Objects and shapes have attributes that can be described, measured, and compared.
- Concrete graphs help us to compare and interpret data and show one-to-one correspondence.

Content (official):

- **1.1** number concepts to 20. Elaboration: counting on and back; skip-counting by 2 and 5; sequence, compare and order to 20; subitizing; base 10; 10 and some more.
- **1.2** ways to make 10. Elaboration: decompose 10 into parts; numbers to 10 can be arranged and recognized; benchmarks of 10 and 20.
- **1.3** addition and subtraction to 20 (understanding of operation and process). Elaboration: decompose 20 into parts; mental math: counting on, making 10, doubles; addition and subtraction are related.
- **1.4** repeating patterns with multiple elements and attributes. Elaboration: identify sorting rules; translate a pattern between representations (orange-blue to circle-square); letter coding; predict an element; patterns with ten-frames and hundred charts; skip-counting patterns on a hundred chart; beading with 3-5 colours.
- **1.5** change in quantity to 20, concretely and verbally. Elaboration: describe a change in quantity (I can build 7 and make it 10 by adding 3).
- **1.6** meaning of equality and inequality. Elaboration: demonstrate and explain equality and inequality; record equations using = and not-equal.
- **1.7** direct measurement with non-standard units (non-uniform and uniform). Elaboration: non-uniform units vary in size (hands, pencils); uniform units are consistent (cubes, paper clips); baseline; multiple copies of a unit; iterate a single unit; tile an area; body parts.
- **1.8** comparison of 2D shapes and 3D objects. Elaboration: sort by one attribute and explain the rule; compare shapes in the environment; positional language (up and down, in and out); replicate composite 2D shapes and 3D objects (two triangles make a square).
- **1.9** concrete graphs, using one-to-one correspondence. Elaboration: create, describe and compare concrete graphs.
- **1.10** likelihood of familiar life events, using comparative language. Elaboration: never, sometimes, always, more likely, less likely.
- **1.11** financial literacy - values of coins, and monetary exchanges. Elaboration: values of nickel, dime, quarter, loonie, toonie; count multiples of the same denomination; money is a medium of exchange; role-play with coins; wants and needs.

### Grade 2

Big Ideas (official):

- Numbers to 100 represent quantities that can be decomposed into 10s and 1s.
- Development of computational fluency in addition and subtraction with numbers to 100 requires an understanding of place value.
- The regular change in increasing patterns can be identified and used to make generalizations.
- Objects and shapes have attributes that can be described, measured, and compared.
- Concrete items can be represented, compared, and interpreted pictorially in graphs.

Content (official):

- **2.1** number concepts to 100. Elaboration: skip-count by 2, 5, 10 from different starting points, forward and backward; compare and order to 100; place value: 10s and 1s, digit value to 99 (the 4 in 49 is 40), decompose 2-digit numbers; even and odd numbers to 100.
- **2.2** benchmarks of 25, 50, and 100 and personal referents. Elaboration: benchmarks of 25, 50, 100 and personal referents.
- **2.3** addition and subtraction facts to 20 (introduction of computational strategies). Elaboration: making or bridging 10, decomposing, related doubles, adding on to find the difference.
- **2.4** addition and subtraction to 100. Elaboration: decompose numbers to 100; estimate sums and differences; friendly numbers (48 + 37: 48 + 2 = 50, 50 + 35 = 85); decompose into 10s and 1s and recompose; compensate; add up to find the difference; open number line, hundred chart, ten-frames; real-life contexts.
- **2.5** repeating and increasing patterns. Elaboration: complex repeating patterns (positional, circular); identify the core; increasing patterns using manipulatives, sounds, actions and numbers (0 to 100).
- **2.6** change in quantity, using pictorial and symbolic representation. Elaboration: numerically describe a change in quantity (6 + n = 10) with ten-frames, hundred charts.
- **2.7** symbolic representation of equality and inequality. Elaboration: symbolic representation of equality and inequality.
- **2.8** direct linear measurement, introducing standard metric units. Elaboration: centimetres and metres; estimate length; measure and record length, height, width in standard units.
- **2.9** multiple attributes of 2D shapes and 3D objects. Elaboration: sort by two attributes and explain the rule; describe, compare, construct triangles, squares, rectangles, circles; 2D shapes as parts of 3D objects.
- **2.10** pictorial representation of concrete graphs, using one-to-one correspondence. Elaboration: collect data, make a concrete graph, represent it with grids, stamps, drawings.
- **2.11** likelihood of familiar life events, using comparative language. Elaboration: certain, uncertain; more, less, or equally likely.
- **2.12** financial literacy - coin combinations to 100 cents, and spending and saving. Elaboration: count simple mixed combinations of coins to 100 cents; introduction to spending and saving; wants and needs; role-play with bills and coins.

### Grade 3

Big Ideas (official):

- Fractions are a type of number that can represent quantities.
- Development of computational fluency in addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of whole numbers requires flexible decomposing and composing.
- Regular increases and decreases in patterns can be identified and used to make generalizations.
- Standard units are used to describe, measure, and compare attributes of objects' shapes.
- The likelihood of possible outcomes can be examined, compared, and interpreted.

Content (official):

- **3.1** number concepts to 1000. Elaboration: skip-count by any number from any start, forward and backward; skip-counting relates to multiplication; place-value counting patterns (698, 699, 700, 701; zero as placeholder); compare and order; estimate large quantities; place value 100s, 10s, 1s (4 in 342 is 40); 0 as a placeholder (408).
- **3.2** fraction concepts. Elaboration: fractions are numbers that represent an amount; parts of a region, set, or linear model; fraction parts are equal shares; concrete materials; pictorial models connected to symbols; equal partitioning.
- **3.3** addition and subtraction to 1000. Elaboration: flexible strategies: taking apart (friendly numbers, compensating), combining, regrouping; estimate sums and differences; real-life contexts; number talks.
- **3.4** addition and subtraction facts to 20 (emerging computational fluency). Elaboration: strategies: decomposing, making and bridging 10, related doubles, commutative property; at the end of Grade 3 most students should recall addition facts to 20.
- **3.5** multiplication and division concepts. Elaboration: multiplication as groups of, arrays, repeated addition; division as sharing, grouping, repeated subtraction; they are related; connect to skip-counting; memorization of facts is not intended.
- **3.6** increasing and decreasing patterns. Elaboration: create with concrete, pictorial, numerical representations; generalize what makes the pattern change (doubling, adding 2).
- **3.7** pattern rules using words and numbers, based on concrete experiences. Elaboration: describe the pattern rule from a concrete pattern using words and numbers.
- **3.8** one-step addition and subtraction equations with an unknown number. Elaboration: start unknown (n + 15 = 20), change unknown (12 + n = 20), result unknown (6 + 13 = n); even and odd numbers with an unknown.
- **3.9** measurement, using standard units (linear, mass, and capacity). Elaboration: cm, m, km; mL, L; g, kg; area in square units (standard and non-standard); estimate with referents; concepts of perimeter, area, circumference (formulas and pi not intended).
- **3.10** time concepts. Elaboration: second, minute, hour, day, week, month, year and relationships between them; estimate time with natural cycles; telling time is not expected at this level.
- **3.11** construction of 3D shapes. Elaboration: identify 3D objects by 2D faces, edges, vertices (nets, skeletons); sphere, cube, prism, cone, cylinder; compare 3D objects; shape is preserved under orientation.
- **3.12** one-to-one correspondence with bar graphs, pictographs, charts, and tables. Elaboration: collect data, create a graph, describe and compare; choose a suitable representation.
- **3.13** likelihood of simulated events, using comparative language. Elaboration: certain, uncertain; more, less, equally likely; chance (coin toss is 50-50); bag, spinner, dice.
- **3.14** financial literacy - fluency with coins and bills to 100 dollars, and earning and payment. Elaboration: total coins and bills up to $100; different combinations make the same amount; flexible payment (cash, cheque, credit, electronic); ways of earning money.

### Grade 4

Big Ideas (official):

- Fractions and decimals are types of numbers that can represent quantities.
- Development of computational fluency and multiplicative thinking requires analysis of patterns and relations in multiplication and division.
- Regular changes in patterns can be identified and represented using tools and tables.
- Polygons are closed shapes with similar attributes that can be described, measured, and compared.
- Analyzing and interpreting experiments in data probability develops an understanding of chance.

Content (official):

- **4.1** number concepts to 10 000. Elaboration: multiples; flexible counting; whole-number benchmarks; compare and order; estimate large quantities; place value 1000s, 100s, 10s, 1s to 10 000.
- **4.2** decimals to hundredths. Elaboration: fractions and decimals are numbers that represent an amount; region, set, linear models; equal shares; the relationship between fractions and decimals.
- **4.3** ordering and comparing fractions. Elaboration: compare and order fractions with common denominators; estimate with benchmarks (zero, half, whole); concrete and visual models; equal partitioning.
- **4.4** addition and subtraction to 10 000. Elaboration: flexible strategies (take apart, friendly numbers, compensate), regrouping; estimate sums and differences to 10 000; real-life contexts.
- **4.5** multiplication and division of two- or three-digit numbers by one-digit numbers. Elaboration: relationships: multiplication and division, multiplication and addition, division and subtraction; flexible strategies (decomposing, distributive, commutative, repeated addition/subtraction); real-life contexts.
- **4.6** addition and subtraction of decimals to hundredths. Elaboration: estimate decimal sums and differences; base 10 blocks, place-value mats, grid paper, number lines; real-life contexts.
- **4.7** addition and subtraction facts to 20 (developing computational fluency). Elaboration: authentic practice; flexible use of mental math strategies.
- **4.8** multiplication and division facts to 100 (introductory computational strategies). Elaboration: concrete and pictorial; connect to skip-counting, division, repeated addition; doubling or halving; memorization not intended; recall 2s, 5s, 10s by end of Grade 4.
- **4.9** increasing and decreasing patterns, using tables and charts. Elaboration: change in patterns is shown in charts, graphs, tables; describe with words and numbers.
- **4.10** algebraic relationships among quantities. Elaboration: represent and explain one-step equations with an unknown; describe pattern rules using words and numbers.
- **4.11** one-step equations with an unknown number, using all operations. Elaboration: start, change, result unknown (___ + 4 = 15, 15 - box = 11).
- **4.12** how to tell time with analog and digital clocks, using 12- and 24-hour clocks. Elaboration: a.m. and p.m.; minutes in an hour; circle and fractions in telling time (half past, quarter to); five-minute intervals; nearest minute.
- **4.13** regular and irregular polygons. Elaboration: describe and sort regular and irregular polygons by multiple attributes.
- **4.14** perimeter of regular and irregular shapes. Elaboration: geoboards and grids: create, represent, measure, calculate perimeter.
- **4.15** line symmetry. Elaboration: pattern blocks to build designs with a mirror image.
- **4.16** many-to-one correspondence, using bar graphs and pictographs. Elaboration: one symbol represents a group or value (one square = five cookies).
- **4.17** probability experiments. Elaboration: predict single outcomes; spinners, dice, bag draws; record results with tallies.
- **4.18** financial literacy - monetary calculations, including making change with amounts to 100 dollars, and simple financial decisions. Elaboration: decimal notation in real-life problems; count up, count back, decompose to total and make change; earning, spending, saving, giving.

### Grade 5

Big Ideas (official):

- Numbers describe quantities that can be represented by equivalent fractions.
- Computational fluency and flexibility with numbers extend to operations with larger (multi-digit) numbers.
- Identified regularities in number patterns can be expressed in tables.
- Closed shapes have area and perimeter that can be described, measured, and compared.
- Data represented in graphs can be used to show many-to-one correspondence.

Content (official):

- **5.1** number concepts to 1 000 000. Elaboration: multiples; flexible counting; benchmarks; compare and order; estimate large quantities; place value 100 000s down to 1s.
- **5.2** decimals to thousandths. Elaboration: decimals to thousandths.
- **5.3** equivalent fractions. Elaboration: two equivalent fractions are two ways to represent the same amount of the same whole.
- **5.4** whole-number, fraction, and decimal benchmarks. Elaboration: compare and order fractions and decimals; estimate fractions with benchmarks (zero, half, whole); equal partitioning.
- **5.5** addition and subtraction of whole numbers to 1 000 000. Elaboration: flexible strategies, regrouping; estimate sums and differences (elaboration text says to 10 000); real-life contexts; number talks to 1 000 000.
- **5.6** multiplication and division to three digits, including division with remainders. Elaboration: relationships among operations; flexible strategies; real-life contexts; number talks to three digits.
- **5.7** addition and subtraction of decimals to thousandths. Elaboration: estimate; base 10 blocks, place-value mats, grid paper, number lines.
- **5.8** addition and subtraction facts to 20 (extending computational fluency). Elaboration: apply facts in context and math-to-math (800 + 700 uses 8 + 7).
- **5.9** multiplication and division facts to 100 (emerging computational fluency). Elaboration: doubling and halving, annexing zeros, distributive property; recall many facts (2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, 10s) by end of Grade 5.
- **5.10** rules for increasing and decreasing patterns with words, numbers, symbols, and variables. Elaboration: identified regularities in number patterns are expressed in tables.
- **5.11** one-step equations with variables. Elaboration: solve; express a problem as an equation with a variable (4 + X = 15).
- **5.12** area measurement of squares and rectangles. Elaboration: tiles, geoboards, grid paper.
- **5.13** relationships between area and perimeter. Elaboration: area and perimeter are related but not dependent on each other.
- **5.14** duration, using measurement of time. Elaboration: elapsed time and duration in real-life problems.
- **5.15** classification of prisms and pyramids. Elaboration: 3D objects and 2D shapes by multiple attributes; sort quadrilaterals; describe and construct rectangular and triangular prisms; prisms and pyramids in the environment.
- **5.16** single transformations. Elaboration: translation (slide), reflection (flip), rotation (turn) with concrete materials.
- **5.17** one-to-one correspondence and many-to-one correspondence. Elaboration: many-to-one on double bar graphs.
- **5.18** probability experiments. Elaboration: predict outcomes of independent events (spinner, dice, bag); represent single-outcome probabilities as fractions.
- **5.19** financial literacy - monetary calculations, including making change and decimal notation to $1000, and simple financial plans. Elaboration: counting up, counting back, decomposing; plan to meet a financial goal; budget with income and expenses.

### Grade 6

Big Ideas (official):

- Mixed numbers and decimal numbers represent quantities that can be decomposed into parts and wholes.
- Computational fluency and flexibility with numbers extend to operations with whole numbers and decimals.
- Linear relations can be identified and represented using expressions with variables and line graphs and can be used to form generalizations.
- Properties of objects and shapes can be described, measured, and compared using volume, area, perimeter, and angles.
- Data from the results of an experiment can be used to predict the theoretical probability of an event and to compare and interpret.

Content (official):

- **6.1** small to large numbers (thousandths to billions). Elaboration: place value from thousandths to billions; operations with thousandths to billions; compare, order, estimate.
- **6.2** multiplication and division facts to 100 (developing computational fluency). Elaboration: mental math (double-double to multiply 23 x 4).
- **6.3** order of operations with whole numbers. Elaboration: includes brackets, excludes exponents; quotients can be rational numbers with whole numbers.
- **6.4** factors and multiples - greatest common factor and least common multiple. Elaboration: prime and composite numbers, divisibility rules, factor trees, prime factorization (300 = 2^2 x 3 x 5^2); Venn diagrams for common factors and multiples.
- **6.5** improper fractions and mixed numbers. Elaboration: benchmarks, number line, common denominators to compare and order, including whole numbers; pattern blocks, Cuisenaire rods, fraction strips, circles.
- **6.6** introduction to ratios. Elaboration: compare numbers and quantities; equivalent ratios; part-to-part and part-to-whole.
- **6.7** whole-number percents and percentage discounts. Elaboration: base 10 blocks, geoboard, 10 x 10 grid; find the missing part or whole; 50% = 1/2 = 0.5 = 50:100; percentage discounts.
- **6.8** multiplication and division of decimals. Elaboration: 0.125 x 3 or 7.2 / 9; base 10 block arrays.
- **6.9** increasing and decreasing patterns, using expressions, tables, and graphs as functional relationships. Elaboration: limited to discrete points in the first quadrant; visual patterning; take 3 add 2 each time, 2n + 1, and 1 more than twice a number all describe 3, 5, 7, ....
- **6.10** one-step equations with whole-number coefficients and solutions. Elaboration: preservation of equality (balance, algebra tiles); 3x = 12, x + 5 = 11.
- **6.11** perimeter of complex shapes. Elaboration: a complex shape is a group of shapes with no holes (colour tiles, pattern blocks, tangrams).
- **6.12** area of triangles, parallelograms, and trapezoids. Elaboration: grid paper explorations; derive formulas; connect parallelogram area to rectangle area.
- **6.13** angle measurement and classification. Elaboration: straight, acute, right, obtuse, reflex; construct and identify; estimate with 45, 90, 180 degrees; angles of polygons.
- **6.14** volume and capacity. Elaboration: build 3D objects with cubes to find volume; referents and relationships between units (cm3, m3, mL, L).
- **6.15** triangles. Elaboration: scalene, isosceles, equilateral; right, acute, obtuse; classified regardless of orientation.
- **6.16** combinations of transformations. Elaboration: plot points on the Cartesian plane (whole-number ordered pairs, first quadrant); translations, rotations, reflections on a single 2D shape.
- **6.17** line graphs. Elaboration: create and interpret a line graph from a table of values.
- **6.18** single-outcome probability. Elaboration: spinner, die, coin; list all possible outcomes for theoretical probability; compare experimental results with theoretical expectation.
- **6.19** financial literacy - informed decision making on saving and purchasing. Elaboration: simple budgeting and consumer math (weeks of allowance to buy a bicycle).

### Grade 7

Big Ideas (official):

- Decimals, fractions, and percents are used to represent and describe parts and wholes of numbers.
- Computational fluency and flexibility with numbers extend to operations with integers and decimals.
- Linear relations can be represented in many connected ways to identify regularities and make generalizations.
- The constant ratio between the circumference and diameter of circles can be used to describe, measure, and compare spatial relationships.
- Data from circle graphs can be used to illustrate proportion and to compare and interpret.

Content (official):

- **7.1** multiplication and division facts to 100 (extending computational fluency). Elaboration: 214 x 5: multiply by 10, then divide by 2 to get 1070.
- **7.2** operations with integers (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and order of operations). Elaboration: concretely, pictorially, symbolically; two-sided counters; 9 - (-4) = 13 because -4 is 13 away from +9; brackets, no exponents.
- **7.3** operations with decimals (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and order of operations). Elaboration: extend whole-number strategies to decimals; brackets, no exponents.
- **7.4** relationships between decimals, fractions, ratios, and percents. Elaboration: conversions, equivalency, terminating versus repeating decimals, place value, benchmarks; compare and order decimals and fractions on a number line; 1/2 = 0.5 = 50% = 50:100.
- **7.5** discrete linear relations, using expressions, tables, and graphs. Elaboration: four quadrants, integral coordinates; 3n + 2: values increase by 3 from y-intercept 2; derive the relation from a graph or table.
- **7.6** two-step equations with whole-number coefficients, constants, and solutions. Elaboration: solve and verify 3x + 4 = 16; preserve equality (balance, algebra tiles).
- **7.7** circumference and area of circles. Elaboration: construct circles from radius, diameter, area, circumference; relationships to derive C = pi x d; A = pi x r x r.
- **7.8** volume of rectangular prisms and cylinders. Elaboration: volume = area of base x height.
- **7.9** Cartesian coordinates and graphing. Elaboration: origin, four quadrants, integral coordinates; links to linear relations and transformations.
- **7.10** combinations of transformations. Elaboration: four quadrants; translations, rotations, reflections, successive transformations; tessellations.
- **7.11** circle graphs. Elaboration: construct, label, interpret; translate percentages in a circle graph into quantities and back.
- **7.12** experimental probability with two independent events. Elaboration: multiple trials (two coins, two dice, spinner twice).
- **7.13** financial literacy - financial percentage. Elaboration: sales tax, tips, discount, sale price.

### Grade 8

Big Ideas (official):

- Number represents, describes, and compares the quantities of ratios, rates, and percents.
- Computational fluency and flexibility extend to operations with fractions.
- Discrete linear relationships can be represented in many connected ways and used to identify and make generalizations.
- The relationship between surface area and volume of 3D objects can be used to describe, measure, and compare spatial relationships.
- Analyzing data by determining averages is one way to make sense of large data sets and enables us to compare and interpret.

Content (official):

- **8.1** perfect squares and cubes. Elaboration: colour tiles, pictures, multi-link cubes; build the number or use prime factorization.
- **8.2** square and cube roots. Elaboration: cube root of 125; square root of 16/169; estimate the square root of 30.
- **8.3** percents less than 1 and greater than 100 (decimal and fractional percents). Elaboration: 122% salary increase problem; half of 1% of 1 billion; 3.25% population increase.
- **8.4** numerical proportional reasoning (rates, ratio, proportions, and percent). Elaboration: two-term and three-term ratios; a string cut in ratio 3:5:7.
- **8.5** operations with fractions (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and order of operations). Elaboration: brackets, no exponents; e.g., simplify 1/2 / 9/6 x (7 - 4/5).
- **8.6** discrete linear relations (extended to larger numbers, limited to integers). Elaboration: two-variable relations; expressions, table of values, graphs; scale values; four quadrants.
- **8.7** expressions - writing and evaluating using substitution. Elaboration: use an expression to describe a relationship; evaluate 0.5n - 3n + 25 if n = 14.
- **8.8** two-step equations with integer coefficients, constants, and solutions. Elaboration: solve and verify 3x - 4 = -12; preserve equality.
- **8.9** surface area and volume of regular solids, including triangular and other right prisms and cylinders. Elaboration: surface area = sum of areas of each side; volume = area of base x height; objects, nets, 3D design software.
- **8.10** Pythagorean theorem. Elaboration: model, derive, find a missing side of a right triangle.
- **8.11** construction, views, and nets of 3D objects. Elaboration: top, front, side views; match a net to its object; construct objects from nets.
- **8.12** central tendency. Elaboration: mean, median, mode.
- **8.13** theoretical probability with two independent events. Elaboration: sample space (tree diagram, table); P(5 on a die and head on a coin) = 1/6 x 1/2 = 1/12; fairness of a spinner game.
- **8.14** financial literacy - best buys. Elaboration: coupons, proportions, unit price; unit rate, equivalent fractions with prices and quantities.

## 2. Strand taxonomy and mapping of all 131 standards

### 2.1 Proposed strands (6, stable across grades 1-7)

| Code | Strand (parent-facing name) | What it holds (K-8 BC content) | BC Big Idea family | Ontario 2020 analogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N | Number sense and place value | Counting and skip-counting, subitizing, comparing and ordering whole numbers, place value (to billions), benchmarks, part-whole decomposition, factors and multiples, squares and roots | "Numbers" (K-2) | B1 Number Sense (whole numbers) |
| O | Operations and computational fluency | Add and subtract, multiply and divide, facts and strategies, estimating results, order of operations, operations with decimals, integers, fractions, money arithmetic | "Computational fluency" (every grade) | B2 Operations |
| F | Fractions, decimals, ratio and percent | Meaning, size and equivalence of fractions and decimals, comparing and ordering them, conversions, ratio, rate, percent, proportional reasoning | "Fractions" (first Big Idea in Gr 3-8) | B1 Number Sense (fractions, decimals, percents) |
| A | Patterns and algebra | Repeating and growing patterns, pattern rules, equality, unknowns, equations, variables, expressions, linear relations | "Patterning" (every grade) | C Algebra |
| MG | Measurement and geometry | Comparing and measuring length, mass, capacity, time, area, perimeter, volume, angles, circles; 2D and 3D shapes, symmetry, transformations, coordinates, nets, Pythagoras | "Geometry and Measurement" (every grade) | E Spatial Sense (E1 geometry, E2 measurement) |
| DP | Data and probability | Graphs (concrete, pictorial, bar, line, circle), tables, likelihood language, experiments, theoretical probability, averages | "Data and Probability" (every grade) | D Data (D1 data, D2 probability) |

### 2.2 Why these six

- **BC already has five stable families.** Every grade K-8 states Big Ideas for number, computational fluency, patterning, geometry and measurement, and data and probability (section 1). The proposed strands follow those five. This keeps the parent report consistent with the language of the BC curriculum and report cards.
- **Number is split in three, because it is 44% of the content.** Number-type standards are N 16 + O 28 + F 14 = 58 of 131. One "Number" score would hide the most useful split for parents: place value (N), computing (O), and fractions and decimals (F). BC itself separates concept from computation: Grade 7 Big Idea 1 is "decimals, fractions, and percents are used to represent and describe parts and wholes", Big Idea 2 is "computational fluency ... operations with integers and decimals". Fraction and division knowledge in Grade 5 predicts high school math achievement, even after controls (Siegler et al. 2012, Psychological Science, doi 10.1177/0956797612440101), so F deserves its own line.
- **Measurement and geometry stay together.** BC pairs them in one Big Idea in every grade. Ontario 2020 also pairs them as "Spatial Sense". Grades 1-3 have only 2-3 standards for them. Optional sub-tags for reporting inside the strand: MG-M (measurement, time) and MG-G (shapes, symmetry, transformations, coordinates). Show the split to parents only if a child has enough items in each part.
- **Financial literacy is not a strand.** BC has one financial literacy standard per grade. In K-3 it is coin recognition and coin arithmetic (number and operations). In Gr 4-6 it is change, budgets (operations). In Gr 7-8 it is percent and unit price (F). One standard per grade gives 2-3 items per child, too few for a reliable strand score. Recommendation: tag every money item `money` and show an optional "money skills" insight computed across strands. (Ontario 2020 has a Financial Literacy strand F; BC does not.)
- **Decision rule for borderline standards.** If the standard asks "what is this number, how big, is it equal" it goes to N or F. If it asks "find the result" it goes to O, whatever the number type (whole, decimal, integer, fraction, money). Standards on graphing a relation go to A. Coordinates and transformations go to MG. Each standard has exactly one strand. A `Note` column shows the standards that also touch a second strand, so item authors can dual-tag.

### 2.5 Where strands are thin or absent (assessment consequences)

- **F is absent in K-2 (0 standards).** BC introduces fractions in Grade 3 (3.2 "fraction concepts"), with no denominator limit stated. For a Grade 1-2 child, F is a readiness probe only (halves, equal shares). Do not report an F score for ages 6-7. Flag F items for Gr 1-2 as "above BC grade".
- **O is absent in K and has one standard in Grade 1 (1.3, add and subtract to 20).** "Ways to make 10" (1.2) and K "change in quantity" (K5, 1.5) are coded N and A, but they behave like early addition. Dual-tag them N+O or A+O in item metadata so a Grade 1 child still gets an O score from 4-5 items.
- **N thins out: 4 standards in K, 3 in Gr 1, 2 in Gr 2, then 1 per grade in Gr 3-5, 2 in Gr 6, 0 in Gr 7, 2 in Gr 8.** Each N standard ("number concepts to X") is large, so do not weight items by standard count. The Grade 7 gap is real: BC has no stand-alone standard for the meaning or ordering of negative numbers. They appear only inside 7.2 "operations with integers". Use N items for negative numbers at Gr 7 and flag them "implied by 7.2".
- **No explicit rounding standard anywhere in K-8.** Estimation appears in elaborations ("estimate sums and differences", "estimating large quantities") and in the Curricular Competency "Estimate reasonably". Rounding to the nearest 10/100 is common in other provinces and U.S. materials. Test estimation (reasonableness), not rounding rules, if the report says "BC aligned".
- **No fraction arithmetic in K-7.** Operations with fractions first appear in Grade 8 (8.5). Grades 3-7 teach fraction meaning, comparison, equivalence (5.3), improper fractions and mixed numbers (6.5), and conversion (7.4) only. Alberta (Gr 5) and Ontario (Gr 5) teach addition and subtraction of fractions earlier (section 5). A fraction-addition item given to a BC Grade 5-7 child is "above BC grade" and must not count as a weakness.
- **No telling time before Grade 4.** Grade 3 says "telling time is not expected at this level" (3.10). Telling time first appears in 4.12. Ontario and Alberta teach it in Grade 3.
- **Multiplication facts come late and are not memorization-first.** 3.5 says "memorization of facts is not intended". Recall target: 2s, 5s, 10s by end of Grade 4; "many" facts (2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, 10s) by end of Grade 5; facts to 100 in "developing/extending fluency" through Grade 7. A timed 12 x 12 fact test would over-penalize BC children in Gr 4-5.
- **Ratio, percent and negative numbers arrive in Grade 6-7.** Ratio and whole-number percent first appear in Grade 6 (6.6, 6.7). Integers first appear in Grade 7 (7.2). Variables in equations first appear in Grade 5 (5.10, 5.11), although 3.8 and 4.11 use an unknown (box or letter).
- **A is steady (2-3 per grade).** It moves from repeating patterns and balance/equality (K-2), to pattern rules and one-step unknowns (3-4), to variables (5), to linear relations and two-step equations (6-8).
- **MG grows from 2 (K-2) to 6 (Grade 6).** It carries the most standards in total (31). Area enters at Grade 3 (square units; perimeter only as a concept), perimeter is calculated from Grade 4, area of squares and rectangles from Grade 5, triangles and parallelograms from Grade 6, circles at Grade 7.
- **DP always has exactly 2 standards.** K-3 content is only graphs (concrete, pictorial, bar) and likelihood words. Experiments start at Grade 4, theoretical probability at Grade 6, and mean/median/mode only at Grade 8 (8.12). Line graphs are Grade 6 and circle graphs Grade 7. A Grade 4-7 child cannot be asked about averages in BC terms. Treat any mean/median item as "above BC grade".

## 5. How close is BC to Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and Ontario?

BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba share one root, the Western and Northern Canadian Protocol (WNCP) Common Curriculum Framework for K-9 Mathematics (May 2006, last amended May 2011): BC's own 2007 K-7 math IRP says its outcomes "are based on" that framework, Manitoba's 2013 K-8 framework is built on it, and Saskatchewan's math outcomes still use its four strands (Number; Patterns and Relations; Shape and Space; Statistics and Probability, codes such as N8.1, P8.1, SS8.1, SP8.1). BC left that model in 2016: the K-9 curriculum is now built from Big Ideas, Curricular Competencies and Content, with few grade-level specifics (no denominator limits, no required algorithm, no timed facts). The sequence of topics is still close to the old WNCP one: fractions start in Grade 3 in BC, Manitoba (Grade 3 overview) and the old Alberta program. Alberta has moved furthest away: its new K-6 math curriculum began in K-3 in September 2022 and in Grades 4-6 in September 2023, and it starts fractions in Grade 1, requires standard algorithms and number-fact recall, teaches telling time and multiplication facts to 10 x 10 in Grade 3, adds and subtracts fractions with common denominators in Grade 5, and multiplies fractions by whole numbers in Grade 6 (Grades 7-9 are still the 2016 WNCP-based program; a draft is in pilot in 2026/27). Ontario's 2020 Grades 1-8 curriculum is organised differently (strands A Social-Emotional Learning and processes, B Number, C Algebra including coding, D Data, E Spatial Sense, F Financial Literacy) and runs earlier than BC on most arithmetic topics: fractions from Grade 1 (halves and fourths through fair sharing), analog and digital clocks in Grade 3, recall of multiplication facts 2/5/10 in Grade 3, 1 x 1 to 10 x 10 in Grade 4 and up to 12 x 12 in Grade 5, whole-number percents in Grade 5, add and subtract fractions with like denominators in Grade 5 and unlike in Grade 6, and integers in Grade 6. The BC equivalents are later: telling time Grade 4; recall of 2s, 5s, 10s by Grade 4 and "many" facts by Grade 5; whole-number percents Grade 6; integers Grade 7; operations with fractions Grade 8. Net effect for placement: strand names and the order of big topics transfer well across the four western provinces, but a test built from Ontario or new-Alberta expectations will make a BC child look behind on fraction arithmetic, time, multiplication-fact recall, percent and integers in Grades 3-7. Tag each item with the BC grade of the matching standard and a flag when BC teaches it at least one grade later than Ontario or Alberta. Limits of this comparison: Saskatchewan's and Manitoba's outcome-level text could not be downloaded (their sites returned a web shell), so those two are compared at the framework and strand level only; Alberta's and Ontario's grade placements come from Alberta Education's own comparison and snapshot pages and from the Ontario 2020 curriculum PDF, read directly.

## 6. Notes for the assessment design

- **Tag every item** with `bcStandard` (ID from section 2.3), `strand`, `bcGrade`, `inBC` (false for fraction arithmetic Gr 3-7, mean/median Gr 4-7, telling time Gr 1-3, rounding rules), `money`.
- **Do not weight strands by standard count.** N is only 16 of 131 standards but each N standard ("number concepts to X") is large and is the base for O and F. Route the adaptive test through N, then O, then F, then the other strands.
- **Report F only from Grade 3 content.** For a Grade 1-2 child, show F as "not yet taught in BC" unless the child places at Grade 3 or above.
- **Report O for Grade 1 from dual-tagged items** (1.2, 1.5 plus 1.3), since 1.3 is the only O standard.
- **Do not test**: rounding rules, timed 12 x 12 facts for Gr 4-5, a single long-division algorithm (BC says flexible strategies), fraction addition before Grade 8 as a BC weakness.
- **Money skills**: nine standards (K11, 1.11, 2.12, 3.14, 4.18, 5.19, 6.19, 7.13, 8.14) are tagged `money`; compute an optional insight from tagged items.
- **Verify the citations in section 4 [R]** before using any claim of the form "research shows" in parent-facing copy. Page-level details are from memory.
- **Repo copy rules apply to anything parent-facing** (AGENTS.md, from DESIGN.md): no proof we do not have, no competitor names, no performance claims. This file names provinces and curricula only as sources of fact.
