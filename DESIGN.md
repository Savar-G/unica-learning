## Job

**User:** A parent in Canada with a child aged 5 to 14. They pay for, or have priced, tutoring or a math app, and they worry about screen time.

**Situation:** They arrive from a Product BC Slack post, a Reddit thread or a direct link, usually on a phone, with under a minute of attention.

**Job to be done:** In 15 seconds, know what this is (math workbooks made for their child, mailed home, that adapt from a photo of the finished pages). Feel that it is a real, serious product. Start the free online math assessment, then choose a plan after the report (D-093).

**Success evidence:** Cards saved after the report, from targeted traffic (pass bar in the research doc: 10+). Secondary: a 5-second test where a parent can say "workbooks made for my kid, mailed home, they adapt".

## Scope

**In:** One landing page whose assessment links open the signup for the free online math assessment (`/start`, D-093), a mobile assessment button, the parent account pages (`/start`, `/sign-in`, `/account`), optional analytics, an "Our story" page (`/our-story`, D-060), a "How we compare" page (`/compare`, D-114), a Resources section of SEO guides for BC parents (`/resources`, linked only from the footer, D-073), supporting 404, thank-you, privacy, and terms pages (D-054), the child's assessment test screens (D-083, D-088), the parent's assessment report in the account and by email (D-084, D-087), a "What's included" page that shows every part of the product as one story (`/whats-included`, D-170; structure draft in `.design/briefs/whats-included.md`), and Parent home with the child's desk (`/tutor`, D-181).

**Out:** Maya's calls (later specs), and a dated blog or news feed.

**Assessment test (the child's screens, D-083, D-088).** Split desk: the question and the answer on the left, the whiteboard on the right, one question per screen. On a laptop or tablet the screen does not scroll (checked at 1440x900, 1024x768 and 768x1024). Every tap target is 44 px or more.
1. New tests use parts of six questions. Each part shows its own progress segments and the current question number. The total length changes with the answers, so the screen gives no final question count before the test ends. A test already in progress keeps its stored stages.
2. The left pane has read-aloud, the question, choices or the number pad, Back, Mark to check again and Next. After 15 seconds, "I don't know yet" becomes available. It selects an explicit unknown response. Next saves it like an answer. The screen never shows right or wrong.
3. The right pane keeps the existing optional whiteboard. The child can draw or use scrap paper. Grades 3 to 9 can also type working.
4. After each part, "Check your answers" shows only that part's questions. The child can change those answers. "Done checking" locks the part before the server selects the next one. A failed save keeps the review open. A retry returns the stored next part.
5. Each review page has "Pause and return later". Answers stay saved for 24 hours after the last saved answer. Returning opens the current question or review. The introduction explains review, unknown answers and pause. It does not ask children to guess.
6. One optional break comes after question 12. Its heading is "Time for a break, [Name]." because it is not a measured halfway point. The existing quick facts follow it. Both keep their saved recovery state.
7. The normal end thanks the child and asks them to pass the device to their grown-up. A test without sufficient evidence shows a separate message asking the grown-up to contact us. It does not produce a starting level or a report.
8. Use the existing split desk, fonts, colours, focus states and 44 px tap targets. Keep Back inside the open part. A review question has "Back to my answers" as its main button.

On a tablet in portrait the panes stack: the question and the answer above, the whiteboard below (at least 360 px high), with multiple choice in two columns.

## Admin area (D-313, D-315)

The admin (`/admin`) is for the team, but it uses the warm look with the same care as the site. Picked prototype: `.design/experiments/r-admin-nav/b-pulse.html`.

- **Frame:** a collapsible left sidebar and the page on the right. The sidebar holds the brand, "Find a family or child", the folders and, at the bottom, "Show test families" and the signed-in email. Folders: Home; Families (Families, Tests, Feedback); Workbooks (Production, Plans and delivery, Marking); Maya (Calls); Marketing (Flyers, Emails); Quality (Templates, Skill map, AI reviews); Team tools (Parent app preview, Export). Items show a count badge only when something waits (orange when it needs the team). Closed, the sidebar is an icon rail; on a phone it is a drawer behind a menu button. Detail pages highlight their folder item.
- **Test families:** one switch, off by default, for every admin page. Off: test and team families are left out of every list and number. On: they show with a dashed "test" tag and stay out of every number. Two exceptions: Templates and Flyers count test families while the switch is on, and say so in a note at the top.
- **Home:** four graphic panels in the team's order: new customers, workbook production, signups, marking. Each panel has one visual (dot chart, stage tracker, funnel and daily bars, ring), its top items, its one-click actions and "See all". A "Since yesterday" timeline follows, and the full number tiles with how they are counted sit in a closed "All numbers" section at the bottom. Home counts real families only; with "Show test families" on, test families can show in its lists, tagged, never in a number.
- **Production:** a stage tracker (No workbook yet, Being made, Ready to review, Approved, Printed, Shipped, Delivered) that filters the page, then groups: Needs you, In progress, Out the door, Done. Check results are plain words; earlier failed or stopped tries sit in a closed line.
- **Families:** a board of families by stage (Signed up, Taking the test, Finished the test, Card saved, Customer) with slim Waitlist and Cancelled lanes below; a family opens its page. Columns hold the dashboard's stages (Finished the test also holds "Payment page", Customer holds the free trial and paying) and count real families only. A card shows the time in its stage, one status line, "New" (a card saved in the last 7 days, as the sidebar badge), "In the test at 2:31 p.m." (a child seen in an open test in the 3 minutes before the page loaded; a time, so a page left open never claims "now") and, with an orange edge, the dashboard's Needs you reason. A column shows its 20 most urgent and recent cards and scrolls inside its own box; "Show all" opens the list. A Board / List switch gives the same families as a table, filtered by stage. On a phone the board scrolls sideways in its own box with 85% wide columns. Maya chat access is set on the family's page.
- **Page pattern:** a mono eyebrow, a light H1, one line that says what the page is for, actions on the right. One spacing scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 px). Cards: 1 px line, 8 px corners. Tables only where people compare rows.
- **Copy:** plain words. No ticket or decision numbers, file paths, environment names, hashes or machine names. Raw data (JSON, ids) sits in a closed "Technical details" section.

## Responsive and accessibility

- Mobile first (375 px), then tablet and desktop (1440 px).
- Phones (below 700 px) get a shorter page (D-072); 700 px and up stay as the page story describes. On phones: the hero photo is a 4:3 crop; How it works shows numbered rows without photos; "Why choose [Brand]?", the drifting-photo banner, the two promise cards, the closing belief cards are hidden; Included drops its photo, puts each icon beside its title, and starts with a "Real paper. Real pencil." card (the Why card 1 text); the FAQ shows six questions and a "More questions" button (44 px) that shows the rest and moves focus to question 7; the cut starts only after JavaScript runs, so without it every question shows. Section order stays the same as desktop, so reading and focus order match. Measured at 390x844: 10.0 screens, pricing on screen 6.6 (was 17.7 and 12.9). The parent section (D-098) shows on phones as the struck-out list with each row stacked, then the card; it takes 1.4 screens (measured 2026-10-12; the four stacked cards of D-075 added 1.3): 11.4 screens, pricing on screen 8.0.
- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast (yellow tiles carry dark text only), visible focus, full keyboard use, alt text that describes the real photo.

## What this is not

- Not mascots, illustrated hills or a cartoon world (Ello's motion yes, its characters no).
- Not dark gradients, glowing app mockups, purple AI palette or sparkle icons.
- Not the round 1 look: muted paper tones and a quiet editorial page.
- Not Primer's system (serif Title Case, hairline grid, flat primary tiles): same industry, too close (D-034).
- Not Polar Signals' own palette or ▲ marker (D-035).
- Not invented proof of any kind.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] In a 15-second test, a parent can say what arrives, who it is for and that it adapts.
- [ ] No competitor brand is named; category comparisons stay general and true (D-064).
- [ ] Every research claim has a citation; no claim about our own results.
- [ ] The assessment path works end to end with all listed states: signup with the email code, the test, the report, then the card (BC) or the waitlist.
- [ ] Rendered evidence at 375, 768 and 1440 px; keyboard pass; reduced-motion pass.
